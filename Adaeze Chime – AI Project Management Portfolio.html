Problems I’ve had to solve

Balancing productivity, quality and client expectations

The most valuable lesson from managing annotation teams is that high productivity doesn’t always mean successful delivery. I met two different performance challenges.

Managing high performers
Some annotators submitted tasks far faster than expected. Speed didn’t necessarily mean inaccurate work, but the client raised concerns about unusually quick submissions and the confidence that created in the output. It showed me why production speed has to be balanced against quality expectations.
Managing low performers
Some team members struggled to meet production expectations, with recurring delays and competing personal commitments affecting their output. That called for closer monitoring, clearer expectations and stronger accountability.

What I took from it

  • Measure productivity alongside quality, never on its own.
  • High output needs quality checks and validation.
  • Monitor individual performance rather than relying on team averages.
  • Distributed teams need clear expectations and accountability.
  • Good project management balances client expectations with the realities of team execution.

How I manage projects

Good project management goes beyond assigning tasks and tracking deadlines. It means understanding the whole workflow, coordinating people well and keeping operational activity aligned with the project’s objectives. Five principles guide me:

  1. Clarity. Team members know the requirements, their responsibilities and what’s expected of them.
  2. Organisation. Clear workflows, task ownership and reporting structures.
  3. Accountability. Monitor progress and address performance gaps early.
  4. Quality. Hold delivery standards while meeting production expectations.
  5. Continuous improvement. Find operational challenges and build practical solutions.
